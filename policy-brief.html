Policy Brief · Sustainable Materials Alliance

Funding Sustainable Materials Innovation — With Accountability

Progress Beyond Plastic

The problem

EPA estimates the United States generated about 35.7 million tons of plastic in municipal solid waste in 2018, of which 8.7% was recycled. NOAA documents that plastic debris such as bags and packing bands can entangle wildlife, restricting movement and feeding and causing injury or death. Because these costs fall on communities and ecosystems beyond the buyer and seller, plastic waste is a negative externality — and research and shared infrastructure are under-financed by the market alone.

Our three requests to Congress

1. Fund competitive research grants for sustainable materials, reusable packaging systems, and recycling improvements. Universities, public research institutions, and qualified innovators would compete through proposals with defined objectives and evaluation criteria.
2. Fund campus and community demonstration grants that test promising solutions under everyday conditions — for example, reusable dining containers paired with accessible return stations.
3. Require measurable results. Grant recipients report costs, participation, waste reduction, and environmental effects. Independent review determines whether projects receive continued support.

Evaluation standards

SolutionMeasures of success
Reusable dining systemsReturn rates, reuse cycles, washing requirements, operating costs, disposables avoided
Improved material designPerformance, affordability, environmental effects, compatibility with local infrastructure
Recycling improvementsMaterial recovered, contamination, energy requirements, cost

SMA evaluates alternatives across production, transportation, use, and disposal. Compostable packaging is supported only where commercial composting collection and processing exist.

Why this is fiscally responsible

Limited pilots, published results, and performance-based renewal ensure that public funds continue only for projects that demonstrate real waste reduction at reasonable cost.
