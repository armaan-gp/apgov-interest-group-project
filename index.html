What this number measures

EPA's most recent national figures for plastic in municipal solid waste — trash and recycling from homes, schools, and businesses. They're historical (2018), not a measure of all plastic production or today's recycling rate. Most non-recycled plastic was landfilled or burned for energy; it didn't all reach the environment.

Wildlife

It doesn't stay where we throw it.

NOAA documents how plastic bags, packing bands, and other marine debris entangle animals — restricting movement and feeding, and potentially causing injury or death.

Campus

It's in our dining halls.

Disposable dining containers, beverage bottles, utensils, and delivery packaging connect this national problem to everyday student life. Campus dining and purchasing decisions are also the perfect place to test what works instead.

Priority

Prevention first.

Reducing unnecessary disposal limits the amount of material that needs collection, processing, or cleanup in the first place.

AP Gov · Why government has a role

Plastic pollution is a negative externality — its costs land on people who never bought the product.

Communities and ecosystems pay for waste they didn't create. That's why SMA supports public investment in research, shared infrastructure, and environmental protection that individual consumers and businesses won't fully finance on their own.

02Our Solutions

Better design. Practical reuse. Effective recovery.

We back technologies on performance, not marketing claims. Every solution has to prove itself with numbers.

A — Reuse

Reusable dining systems

Durable containers circulate through distribution, convenient return stations, washing, and repeated use — replacing thousands of single-use clamshells.

B — Design

Improved material design

Research into packaging that uses less material or is easier to recover after use — while staying affordable and actually working.

We measure
  • Performance
  • Affordability
  • Environmental effects
  • Fits local infrastructure
C — Recovery

Recycling improvements

Better sorting and processing technologies that recover useful materials — supported only when independent evidence shows improved recovery at reasonable cost.

We measure
  • Material recovered
  • Contamination
  • Energy use
  • Cost

Example: why "compostable" isn't automatically better

Commercially compostable plastic only breaks down in a commercial or industrial composting facility — not in a backyard bin or a landfill. EPA advises choosing it only where a local composting program actually accepts it. So we support compostable packaging only where that collection and processing exist.

03Policy Agenda

Three asks for Congress.

Turning promising ideas into public policy — with accountability built in.

  1. 1

    Fund competitive research grants.

    For sustainable materials, reusable packaging systems, and recycling improvements. Universities, public research institutions, and qualified innovators compete with defined goals and evaluation criteria.

    Administered by: Department of Energy
  2. 2

    Test solutions on real campuses.

    Demonstration grants for limited campus and community pilots — like reusable dining containers paired with accessible return stations, with participation and waste reduction measured.

    Administered by: EPA
  3. 3

    Require results.

    Grant recipients report costs, participation, waste reduction, and environmental effects. Independent review decides whether a project keeps its funding.

    Applies to: every funded project
Congress

Authorizes programs and appropriates the money. The target of our lobbying.

Dept. of Energy

Would run technology-research grants under our proposal.

EPA

Would run waste-reduction demonstration grants under our proposal.

Agency roles are SMA's proposed framework, not an existing legislative package. Limited trials and performance requirements answer concerns about public spending: money continues only where results are demonstrated.

05Campus Reuse Challenge

Start with one campus. Build evidence for wider change.

06Take Action

Make sustainable innovation a policy priority.

  • Grassroots lobbying

    Contact Congress

    Members send personalized letters and emails, show up at town halls, and join district-office meetings — all asking for our three specific funding priorities.

  • Direct lobbying

    Meet policymakers

    SMA requests congressional-office meetings and presents a concise policy brief combining published evidence with our campus findings.

  • Coalition building

    Build a coalition

    Prospective partners: university researchers, environmental organizations, campus sustainability offices, and credible sustainable-tech businesses — each bringing expertise, implementation, or organizing experience.

Dear [Representative / Senator Name], I am a constituent from [City, State] and a supporter of the Sustainable Materials Alliance. In 2018, the United States generated about 35.7 million tons of plastic in municipal solid waste, and only 8.7% was recycled. Please support competitive funding for sustainable-materials research and campus waste-reduction demonstrations, with independent evaluation and public reporting of results. Programs like these would let universities and communities test practical solutions — such as reusable dining-container systems — and keep funding only the projects that show measurable results. Thank you for your consideration. Sincerely, [Your Name] [Your Address]
